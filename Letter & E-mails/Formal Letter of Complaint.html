Overview & Writing Tips

TIP

When we write a formal letter of complaint about a problem (e.g. something we have bought, the services in a restaurant or hotel, etc.), we divide it into three parts:

  • In the introduction, we state the reason for writing, (i.e. say what/who we are complaining about and why).
  • In the main body, we state our complaints and give examples and/or reasons to support them. We use a separate paragraph for each topic.
  • In the conclusion, we state what action we expect to be taken.

*The main body can consist of one or more paragraphs, depending on the number of specific complaints we have.*

Important

The tone of our letter must be polite and we should never be rude.

Purpose

A formal complaint letter aims to resolve an issue by clearly stating problems and requesting specific actions from the responsible party.

Tone

Maintain a professional, respectful tone. Be firm but polite. Avoid emotional language, accusations, or threats.

Effectiveness

Include specific details: dates, names, product/service information. Provide evidence when possible (receipts, photos).

Letter Structure

A well-structured complaint letter follows a clear three-part format. Each section has a specific purpose and content requirements.

1 Introduction

The opening paragraph sets the context for your complaint.

  • State the reason for writing immediately
  • Identify what/who you are complaining about
  • Briefly mention why you're dissatisfied
  • Include relevant details: date, location, product/service

Example: "I am writing to express my dissatisfaction with..."

2 Main Body

This section contains the details of your complaint.

  • State specific complaints clearly
  • Use separate paragraphs for each main point
  • Provide examples and evidence
  • Explain the impact or consequences
  • Maintain logical flow between paragraphs

This can be 1-3 paragraphs depending on complexity.

3 Conclusion

The closing paragraph states your expected resolution.

  • Clearly state what action you expect
  • Set a reasonable deadline for response
  • Provide contact information
  • Thank the reader for their attention
  • Mention next steps if unresolved

Example: "I expect a full refund within 14 days..."

Letter Template

Use this template as a guide for structuring your formal complaint letter. Fill in the bracketed information with your specific details.

Formal Complaint Letter Template

Dear Sir/Madam,

Introduction

Paragraph 1:

  • State your reason for writing
  • Identify what/who you're complaining about
  • Mention when and where the issue occurred

[e.g., "I am writing to complain about the poor service I received at your restaurant on [date]..."]

Main Body

Paragraphs 2 - 4:

  • List your specific complaints
  • Provide examples and supporting details
  • Explain how you were affected
  • Use separate paragraphs for different issues

[e.g., "Firstly, the food arrived cold... Secondly, the waiter was rude... Finally, the bill contained errors..."]

Conclusion

Final paragraph:

  • State what action you expect
  • Set a deadline for response
  • Provide your contact details
  • Thank them for their attention

[e.g., "I expect a full refund within 14 days. Please contact me at [phone/email] to resolve this matter..."]

Yours faithfully,

[Your full name]

Key Points & Examples

Planning Your Complaint Letter

Before writing, organize your thoughts using this plan:

Dear Sir/Madam,

Introduction

Paragraph 1:

  • reason for writing

Main Body

Paragraphs 2 - 4:

  • complaints (with examples and/or reasons)

Conclusion

Final paragraph:

  • action to be taken

Yours faithfully

...... (your full name)

Do's

  • Use formal language and proper salutations
  • Be specific with dates, times, names
  • Keep copies of all correspondence
  • State desired outcome clearly
  • Proofread for spelling/grammar errors

Don'ts

  • Use emotional or aggressive language
  • Make personal attacks or threats
  • Forget to include contact information
  • Be vague about the problem or solution
  • Send without checking facts first

Remember

A formal complaint letter is more effective when it's:

  • Clear: The reader should immediately understand the issue
  • Concise: Get to the point without unnecessary details
  • Constructive: Focus on finding a solution, not just complaining
  • Courteous: Maintain professionalism throughout
